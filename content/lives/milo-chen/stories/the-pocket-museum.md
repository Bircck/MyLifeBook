The museum began in a coat pocket and moved to a shoebox after an incident involving the washing machine.

Milo made labels. A smooth stone became “STONE THAT LOOKS LIKE A POTATO”. A piece of green glass, checked carefully by his dad, became “MAYBE TREASURE”. The important stick needed no explanation, although he provided one anyway: it looked exactly like a smaller tree.

On Saturday he arranged everything on the kitchen floor. The entrance was between two chairs. Visitors received a ticket made from a cereal packet and were asked to walk slowly because this was a museum.

His little cousin picked up the potato stone immediately. Milo considered closing the museum. Instead he made a new section called “THINGS YOU CAN TOUCH”, with the stone as its first exhibit.

There was a café too. It served water in very small cups and one biscuit per person. Milo’s dad asked whether he could have another biscuit. The director said the café had already closed.

The museum went back into its box before supper, except for the stick, which was too long. That stayed on the windowsill.

The next morning Milo found a feather on the way to school. He stopped so suddenly that his dad nearly walked into him. An entire new department had just become possible.

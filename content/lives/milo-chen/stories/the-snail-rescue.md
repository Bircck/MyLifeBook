Milo found the snail in the middle of the pavement, apparently on its way somewhere that would take a very long time.

He crouched down. His mum crouched beside him. The snail continued with its plans.

“Do you think it knows about bikes?” he asked.

They decided a leaf might be a useful bridge to the planted strip beside the path. Milo held one in front of the snail. The snail went around it. He tried a larger leaf. The snail considered this for several minutes, during which a dog came to inspect the inspectors.

Eventually it climbed aboard. Milo carried the leaf with both hands, walking more carefully than he had ever walked while carrying a full drink. He put it down beside a damp plant and waited until the snail began to leave.

At home he drew a map of the rescue. The pavement was grey. The leaf was green. The snail was orange because the brown pencil had disappeared under the sofa.

He wanted to add an arrow showing where the snail went next, but his mum said they did not know.

Milo thought about that, then drew three arrows and a question mark. On the back he wrote: “SUCCESSFUL, PROBABLY.”

They took the same route the following morning. Naturally, they checked the pavement.

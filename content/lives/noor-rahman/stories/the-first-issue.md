The first issue was eight pages, including the cover, which I insisted counted as a page. It was called *Side Notes*. I thought of the title while I was supposed to be doing something else, so that felt accurate.

I asked three friends for anything they had made. I got a drawing of a pigeon, a poem with no title, and a photograph of the floor of a bus. Perfect, actually.

I added a list of things overheard that week and a tiny review of the sandwich shop. The review was mostly about the man who remembers everybody’s order. Five stars for remembering. The sandwich was fine.

Printing took ages because I put the paper in the wrong way twice. We folded the copies on my bedroom floor. The stapler could not reach the middle, so we opened it flat and stapled into a piece of cardboard. This felt like discovering a new branch of engineering.

I left five copies at the café, with permission. Then I spent two days trying not to check whether anyone had taken one.

By Friday, three were gone. One might have been used to stop a table wobbling. I choose to believe otherwise.

There is a spelling mistake on page six. Issue two will probably have a different one.

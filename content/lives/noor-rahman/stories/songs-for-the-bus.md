I make playlists with extremely specific purposes. Walking somewhere when you are already late. Cleaning your room but mostly sitting on the floor. The bus home when it is dark earlier than you expected.

That last one is the longest. It started with six songs and now takes longer than the actual journey, which is useful because the bus does not always feel committed to the timetable.

There is a seat on the left where the window does not rattle. If it is free, I take it. The route passes a laundrette, two closed shops, a bright little grocery, and a house where someone always leaves the kitchen curtains open. I know this sequence better than I know some things I have revised for exams.

One evening my headphones stopped working. I spent the first few minutes annoyed. Then I heard two people behind me trying to remember the name of a film. They described nearly the entire plot without getting there.

I knew the answer. I said it just before my stop. They both cheered, quite quietly, because it was still a bus.

I added a note to the playlist when I got home: “Occasionally take the headphones off.”

It is not a song. It gets its own place anyway.

The invitation was handwritten and taped inside the building’s front door. “Dinner in the courtyard, Saturday at six. Bring a chair if you can.” Sam added their flat number, then spent ten minutes deciding whether a smiley face made it more welcoming or less credible.

At five thirty, it began to rain.

They moved two tables into the wide entrance hall and propped the courtyard door open. Bea brought a salad. A man from upstairs brought six chairs, one at a time, explaining that he had expected this might happen.

Twelve people came. Sam had spoken to most of them before, but usually while carrying rubbish or looking for a parcel. Sitting down required a different conversation.

There were awkward gaps. Someone’s child filled several of them by describing a dream about a giant supermarket. People passed bread while they thought of things to say.

By the time the rain stopped, nobody wanted to move the tables again.

The dinner did not transform the building overnight. People still disagreed about the bicycles in the hall. But names began to replace descriptions. “The person on the second floor” became Arun. “The woman with the dog” became Bea, who was already wondering when they might do it again.

Sam kept the invitation. The smiley face had stayed. Underneath, somebody had written: “Thanks. Next time I’ll make dessert.”

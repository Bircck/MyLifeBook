The blue pot has moved with Sam six times. It has a chip near one handle and a lid that must be rotated slightly to sit flat. Both details are included in the instructions when someone else cooks.

Sunday cooking began in a small shared flat, when four people were trying to spend less on lunch. Sam made a large pot of beans. Everyone took a portion to work. By Wednesday, nobody wanted beans again, so the following week someone suggested soup.

Over time, lunch preparation became dinner. A friend came to collect a container and stayed. Another brought bread. Someone who did not cook became exceptionally reliable about washing up.

Now Sam starts after lunch, without knowing exactly how many people will come. There is a message in the group chat: “Pot’s on.” The rest is negotiated through doorbells.

The recipe changes. There are always onions. Beyond that, the cupboard gets a vote.

A friend once asked Sam to write it down properly. They tried. The page began with measurements, then drifted into “until it smells right” and “more liquid if needed”. In the margin, Sam added the part they were sure about: make enough for tomorrow.

There are quieter Sundays too, when nobody comes. Sam eats a bowl by the window, fills a few containers, and sends the same message the following week.

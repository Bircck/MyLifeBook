Sam labelled every box carefully until the last afternoon. After that, labels included “ODDS”, “MORE ODDS”, and one optimistic “DEAL WITH LATER”.

At the new flat, the boxes marked kitchen went into the kitchen. Unfortunately, this was most of the boxes. Nobody could remember which one contained the kettle.

Their friend Jo suggested ordering food. Sam insisted tea should come first. This was less a preference than a way of making the unfamiliar room begin to belong to them.

They found the kettle wrapped in a towel, the mugs inside the blue pot, and the tea in a box labelled books. Milk had to be borrowed from the neighbour, whom Sam met while holding a completely empty measuring jug.

Her name was Bea. She lent them the milk and explained which window stuck when it rained. This seemed useful information of the sort no contract ever included.

They ate on the floor that evening. From there, the room looked mostly like cardboard. The one unpacked lamp made a small circle of yellow light around the food.

Jo asked whether Sam thought they would like living there.

“I think so,” Sam said, although they did not know yet.

The jug went back the following morning, filled with flowers from the corner shop. Bea opened the door, looked at it, and said she had been wondering where she put that.

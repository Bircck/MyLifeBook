# Fictional demo collections

Created 2026-09-19 for the implementation session. Every person, event, relationship, quotation, and memory in `content/lives/` is invented. The names do not identify real subjects. No founder vault, family material, recording, interview, personal album, or private correspondence was used. The quoted speech is fictional dialogue, not testimony.

The prose is original agent-composed demo material contributed to this repository under its MIT license. Illustrative photographs have their own licenses and provenance; see the [media provenance record](../content/ASSET-LICENSES.md). They depict atmosphere or objects, not these fictional people or proof that these events happened. Story source metadata preserves that distinction. Real imports must retain their actual source attribution and editorial treatment instead of borrowing these demo labels.

## Editorial choices

| Collection | Reading question | Voice and organisation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ellen Falk, 1938–2024 | Can remembrance feel particular without becoming a formal biography? | Five small memories, third person, about attention, food, repair, travel, and a garden. Mostly undated; the creator’s order is intentional. |
| Milo Chen, age 8 | Can a life collection stay open, playful, and unfinished? | Three observations from childhood: a pocket museum, a snail rescue, and questions at bedtime. |
| Noor Rahman, age 17 | Can the subject sound like they own the page? | Three imagined first-person entries about a zine, skating, and a bus playlist. No real lyrics or personal messages. |
| Sam Rivera, age 42 | Can an ordinary adult life have substance without being a résumé? | Three memories about a cooking pot, moving home, and a first neighbourhood dinner. |

These are editorial experiments, not researched personas or claims about how people of these ages should sound. Ages and themes help compare presentations; neither is a compulsory authoring structure. No achievement list, date, chapter, or life-stage questionnaire is needed to make a story publishable.

`life.json` supplies the profile and optional presentation hints. Each Markdown file is a complete, independently editable story. `storyOrder` expresses the creator’s chosen reading order. Individual stories have source metadata and explicit publication access. Do not copy a demo profile into a real biography without replacing all invented text and confirming permissions for the material.

## Selected editions

- `ellen-remembrance` selects the first three public Ellen stories in their default order.
- `ellen-garden` selects “What the garden knows”, then “The long way home”. It intentionally changes the order and excludes the table, mending, and travel stories.

The different selections test reuse of the same story files rather than a second edited copy. A public collection is a publication decision, not an access-controlled family website. A selected edition must omit unselected stories and their exclusive media from its generated output.

## Privacy fixtures

`a-letter-kept.md` is explicitly private and absent from Ellen’s public `storyOrder`. It contains no personal information. The distinct story marker is `MYLIFEBOOK_PRIVATE_STORY_CANARY_ELLEN_8F3C`; its exclusive SVG media contains `MYLIFEBOOK_PRIVATE_ASSET_CANARY_ELLEN_7D2B`. These fixtures allow generated HTML, data, downloads, print outputs, and media trees to be checked for unintended exposure.

The synthetic fixture itself is deliberately committed to the public source repository. That is appropriate only because it is artificial test data. Real restricted stories and assets must not be committed to a public repository, and excluding them from a website build does not protect the source repository.

## Limitations

The short fictional collections exercise reading and selection; they do not validate real-world authorship, memory accuracy, import fidelity, privacy controls, multilingual support, or contributor permissions. Shared illustrative images in the smaller profiles demonstrate layout with modest assets, not full personal albums. Add real voices only with informed permission and accurate source labels.
